Panel 1: Oligonucleotide-specific considerations and where the documents address them

  1. Hybridisation-dependent off-target effects. Sequence-complementary off-target binding. Addressed in: FDA oligo nonclin.; OSWG papers; JP oligo nonclin..
  2. Immunostimulation (TLR / pro-inflammatory). Innate-immune activation (CpG/TLR9), complement. Addressed in: CNS delivery review; FDA oligo nonclin.; OSWG papers; JP oligo nonclin..
  3. Hepatotoxicity. Dose-limiting liver findings for several chemistries. Addressed in: CNS delivery review; FDA DILI; FDA oligo nonclin..
  4. Renal accumulation / nephrotoxicity. Proximal-tubule accumulation, especially PS backbones. Addressed in: FDA oligo nonclin.; FDA renal PK.
  5. Thrombocytopenia (platelet class effect). Platelet declines seen with some 2'-MOE/PS ASOs. Addressed in: FDA oligo clin-pharm; FDA oligo nonclin..
  6. Oligonucleotide impurity control. n-1/n+1, shortmers, depurination, process-related impurities. Addressed in: EMA IMPD-Q; EMA oligo GL; ICH Q11; ICH Q3A(R2); ICH Q3C(R9); ICH Q6A; JP oligo quality; Ph. Eur. gen. texts; USP oligo prog..
  7. Delivery & conjugation. GalNAc, antibody conjugates (AOC), LNP, peptide carriers. Addressed in: EMA drug–device GL; EMA mAb GL; FDA ADC clin-pharm; FDA liposomes; FDA nanomaterials; FDA excipient pilot; ICH S6(R1) · AOC.
  8. CNS delivery / intrathecal administration. IT/ICV dosing, neurotoxicity, local tolerance. Addressed in: IT procedure effects; CNS delivery review; IT NHP lesions; IT nerve-fibre opinion; EMA drug–device GL; 21 CFR 4 combination.
  9. Clinical pharmacology profile. Tissue PK, low CYP involvement, hepatic/renal impairment, QT, immunogenicity. Addressed in: FDA oligo clin-pharm; E14/S7B Q&A; ICH M10 bioanalysis.

Scope — what this map covers and what it leaves out on purpose

In scope

  • Synthetic oligonucleotide actives: antisense (incl. gapmers and splice-switching), siRNA, aptamers; conjugated (GalNAc, antibody, peptide) and lipid-nanoparticle-formulated forms.
  • Jurisdictions: ICH, EU centralised procedure and Clinical Trials Regulation, US FDA and federal statute, Japan MHLW/PMDA oligonucleotide texts, UK trial authorisation.
  • Development from candidate selection to post-approval obligations arising from paediatric and conditional-approval commitments.
  • Non-binding consensus (OSWG, Toxicologic Pathology Forum, review articles) where regulators are known to rely on it and no binding text exists — marked as such.

Out of scope (deliberately)

  • mRNA and gene-editing products (own regulatory frameworks, ATMP in the EU), except where a document explicitly covers both.
  • China NMPA, Health Canada, Australia TGA, Swissmedic and other national frameworks.
  • Pharmacovigilance, risk-management plans, labelling, pricing and reimbursement, GMP inspection law, environmental risk assessment.
  • Product-specific precedent (assessment reports, labels) — a possible future node type, listed under open decisions.

Reading the verification fields

  • Source opened: the official page or PDF was read during the check.
  • URL on official site: the document was located on the issuing body’s domain but not opened.
  • Secondary source opened: a non-official source (law-firm summary, library record) was read because the primary text could not be reached.
  • Not checked live: carried from prior knowledge. As of 28 October 2026 no document is in this state.
  • Negative results are recorded with the date they were established, so that “nothing found” is distinguishable from “not looked”.

What changed in data v0.3 (28 October 2026)

  • Data model: every node carries domain, gate_from/gate_to, a unique key, optional group, triggers and last_checked; phase and stored governs_phase edges are gone (gate links are regenerated at build time under derived). Figure placement and series aggregation now come from the data; the renderer holds no layout facts.
  • Content: the intrathecal/CNS gap is closed with four consensus and methodology references (two opened and cited in full); hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity each gained an FDA clinical guidance; revision history added (Q1A(R2) → consolidated Q1, E6(R2) → E6(R3), E9 + E9(R1), 2014 expedited-programs guidance → 2024 accelerated-approval draft).
  • Verification: the four US statutes were re-verified on primary govinfo texts; ICH Q14 on the EMA page; the sponsor-investigator IND draft via its Federal Register notice; no document remains “not checked live”. EDQM newsroom and Pharmeuropa 38.2 confirmed the absence of an oligonucleotide chapter (negative result, dated).
  • Tooling: validate.py (schema, integrity, unique keys, span and group consistency, orphan check, optional link report) and a CI workflow that runs it on every change and the link check quarterly.
  • Second pass (same day): Korte et al. 2020 fully cited; USP negative result dated; EMA drug–device guideline and 21 CFR Part 4 added for device-delivered CNS programmes; accelerated assessment and conditional MA spans start at Phase 3; Rare Pediatric Disease voucher linked to the govinfo text of Pub.   • Page: Hide now also applies to programme-type selections and collapses empty rows and lanes; “Table follows filters” shows its state and the row count; superseded documents are drawn struck through.

Audit of data v0.2 (28 October 2026, before the v0.3 changes above), measured on the graph itself

Structure

  • No domain dimension. 50 of 92 nodes are typed guideline, which does not separate quality from nonclinical from clinical. 26 nodes sat in a cross_cutting phase bucket that carries no information. The map above introduces six domains instead.
  • Phase is a point, applicability is a span. 64 documents had governs_phase edges, only 7 to more than one gate; 22 converged on “filing” and 14 on “nonclinical”, making those milestones hubs with no discriminating value. The map replaces the edges by placement across gates.
  • Modality is almost unused. 81 of 92 nodes carry all_oligo; programme-type triggers (AOC, LNP, intrathecal, individualised, paediatric oncology) existed only in prose. The highlight buttons above are a first mapping and belong in the data.
  • Edge mix. 73 of 148 relations were governs_phase; the remaining references, amends and addresses edges are the ones worth drawing, and the explorer should show only those.

Content

  • Intrathecal / CNS delivery is a consideration with no document addressing it — the most important gap for paediatric brain-tumour programmes.
  • Hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity each hang on a single document (FDA oligo nonclinical draft).
  • 8 documents are not checked live, among them the four US statutes (RACE, PREA, BPCA, Orphan Drug Act) whose links follow URL patterns rather than opened pages.
  • Ph. Eur. and USP have no dedicated oligonucleotide chapter that could be confirmed; that negative result is itself a finding worth stating.
  • Two designations are drawn as applying at filing only (accelerated assessment, conditional MA) although their strategy starts earlier; spans should be reviewed by someone who has run them.

Presentation (previous explorer)

  • Force layout is non-deterministic: positions differ on every load, so the view cannot serve as a reference figure.
  • Colour carried jurisdiction, but the decision-relevant variable is status: ICH is 96% in force, US documents are 27% drafts.
  • Labels averaged 45 characters; keys of ≤22 characters are needed for a figure and a citable register.

Roadmap — from reference map to maintained open resource

Phase A · Data model v0.3 done · 28 Oct 2026

Goal: one source of truth from which both the map and the explorer are generated, with no layout decisions hidden in renderer code.
